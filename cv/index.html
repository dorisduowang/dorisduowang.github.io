Professional Summary

I study strategic interaction under incomplete information. My current public goods experiment examines how announced enforcement changes cooperation before any punishment occurs. A matched-team study holds private evidence fixed and changes the group. My operations work holds firm roles fixed and changes agent capability. The broader agenda is institutional design for settings in which AI agents act for people or firms.

    Does group stability sustain cooperation more reliably than peer punishment or institutional enforcement?

Working Papers

  • Language Monitoring and Automated Enforcement in Public Goods Games. Designed a randomized comparison across 60 blocks and 180 fresh openings. Adding a language-triggered enforcement channel reduced first-3-round group earnings by 16.45% relative to a recording-only monitor, before punishment was available. The comparison identifies the effect of the announced arrangement. The roles of own-message control and an additional enforcement route remain to be separated.
  • News Metadata and Market Volatility Around FOMC Meetings. Built distributed TF-IDF processing for more than 2 million GDELT metadata records around an 88-meeting calendar. The writing sample uses 33 meetings, 5 strictly later returns, HC3 OLS, and 20,000 permutations across 7 archived dates.
