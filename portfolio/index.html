Cooperation in Large-Scale Repeated Games

1.5M+ behavioral records in the Ikariam source

My King's College London dissertation asks when peer punishment can sustain cooperation as groups change over time.

Study design · Track fixed and regrouped players across rounds. Study details
Research question
How do group stability, peer punishment, and institutional enforcement shape contributions in repeated public goods games?
Data
The pooled dataset contains 12,059 observations drawn from Ikariam game records and a controlled public goods experiment.
Methods
I harmonized the two datasets and used pooled regressions with interaction terms and cluster-robust standard errors to compare grouping and enforcement conditions.

2000–2020 daily gold price data

Data

LLM Washing Machine

Sparse features representation analysis

How do language models represent a compound concept such as “washing machine”? I compare it with its head word using similarity and sparse-feature analyses.

Study design · Compare “washing machine” with “machine” alone. Study details
Research question
Does GPT-2 encode compound concepts like “washing machine” through dedicated features, through their component words, or through both?
Data
Twenty-one hand-curated compounds ranging from transparent phrases to idioms, with matched prompts for their component words.
Methods
I compared GPT-2 Small activations using cosine similarity, measured sparse autoencoder feature overlap, and examined next-token probabilities.

About me

Hi, I’m Doris.

I’m an M.A. student in Computational Social Science (Economics) at the University of Chicago. I received my B.Sc. in Political Economy from King’s College London in 2025.

In round 10 of a game I ran with 4 AI agents, one player asked everyone to put 5 tokens into a shared pot and then put in 10 itself. I paired the players’ messages with their contributions over 15 rounds and scored the messages to see what they could tell me about the next round once I knew whether punishment would be active. I used the same pairing of talk and action to design a comparison for GRAIL, where teams of 5 AI agents choose a city together. I vary who sits on each team, keeping the private report assigned to each seat and the information made public fixed across teams. The design lines up the facts that come up in each discussion with the team’s final choice.
