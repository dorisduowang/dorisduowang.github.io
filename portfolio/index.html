Research agenda

How do rules shape strategic behavior?

I study strategic interaction under incomplete information. The central question is how the rules of a market or organization change the way participants use private information. My current public goods experiment studies this through communication and costly enforcement. The projects that follow carry the same question into group choice and supply chain bargaining.

1. Monitor action 2. Hold information fixed 3. Change capability 4. Design the institution
  1. SignalWhat information enters the conversation?
  2. RuleWhat do actors know about monitoring and enforcement?
  3. AllocationWho can use the information, and who gains bargaining power?
  4. OutcomeDoes the group coordinate, choose well, or shift surplus?

Monitoring and enforcement

When words can trigger punishment

Across 60 randomized blocks, announcing an additional language-triggered enforcement channel reduced opening group earnings by 16.45% relative to a recording-only monitor. All 3 observed rounds had punishment switched off. The remaining question is whether the loss depends on linking the group's own messages to enforcement or on the additional route to punishment.

Information aggregation

Does the same evidence produce a different group decision?

The public goods game pairs talk with individual action. GRAIL moves the same question to a team. I designed 20 matched deliberations with 5 AI agents. Each agent received a fixed private report. The group then discussed the evidence and chose a city.

Read the public GRAIL protocol

Who sits on each team.
What stays fixed
The report each seat receives and the information made public.
What I compare
The facts each team discusses, participation inside the group, and the final city.

Agentic Supply Chains

I test whether unequal AI capabilities shift bargaining power across a multi-tier supply chain when every firm keeps the same role and information boundary.

Question, data and method
Research question
When firms use agents of unequal capability, who captures the surplus and where does coordination fail?
Data
The experiment records decisions across a 4-tier economy while preserving each firm's permitted observations and operating constraints.
Methods
I hold role instructions fixed, vary agent capability, and compare prices, quantities, agreement, and surplus across network positions.

Which Reviews Identify Quality?

I study whether the reviews people prefer are also the reviews that contain the most useful evidence about product quality.

Question, data and method
Research question
Are negative reviews more precise and more useful for identifying quality than positive reviews?
Data
Three preregistered experiments include 977 participants and 468 review texts.
Methods
I reconstructed the reported analyses, compared independent R and Python pipelines, and measured linguistic evidence in each review.

Cooperation in Large-Scale Repeated Games

My King's College London dissertation asks when peer punishment can sustain cooperation as groups change over time.

Question, data and method
Research question
How do group stability, peer punishment, and institutional enforcement shape contributions in repeated public goods games?
Data
The pooled dataset contains 12,059 observations drawn from Ikariam game records and a controlled public goods experiment.
Methods
I harmonized the two datasets and used pooled regressions with interaction terms and cluster-robust standard errors to compare grouping and enforcement conditions.

Long-term direction

Markets and organizations where AI agents act for people and firms

The next problem is not whether an agent is intelligent. It is which rules make its information useful without giving the strongest agent control over the outcome. AI systems will hold private information and negotiate across a network. Some will also learn how enforcement works. I want institutions that preserve coordination under those conditions.

Auditing Text-Based Reward Signals

I examined how alternative reward signals change which papers an AI research system surfaces.

Question, data and method
Research question
Can rewards for surprising language help an AI system surface high-impact scientific papers?
Data
The course audit covered 443 publications, with links to researcher profiles and scientific concepts.
Methods
I built a directed hypergraph and simulated three researcher personas, then compared ranking stability and the recovery of high-impact papers across reward rules.
Read the project

Gold Forecasting with ARIMA and LSTM

I compare whether classical and neural models can forecast gold returns on data they have not seen.

Question, data and method
Research question
How well do ARIMA and LSTM forecast gold returns on unseen data?
Data
Daily US-dollar gold prices from Yahoo Finance from January 2000 through April 2020, with May 1, 2020 held out for forecasting.
Methods
I fitted ARIMA and LSTM under competing specifications and compared forecast errors using mean absolute error and root mean squared error.
Read the paper

Gender Pay and Structural Inequality

An analysis of UK gender pay disparities using census microdata, regression and model diagnostics.

Question, data and method
Research question
How does the estimated UK gender pay gap change after accounting for education and job tenure?
Data
An analytical sample of UK census microdata with wages and worker characteristics.
Methods
I compared the unadjusted pay gap with estimates from multivariable wage regressions and checked for multicollinearity and unequal error variance.

LLM Washing Machine

How do language models represent a compound concept such as “washing machine”? I compare it with its head word using similarity and sparse-feature analyses.

Question, data and method
Research question
Does GPT-2 encode compound concepts like “washing machine” through dedicated features, through their component words, or through both?
Data
Twenty-one hand-curated compounds ranging from transparent phrases to idioms, with matched prompts for their component words.
Methods
I compared GPT-2 Small activations using cosine similarity, measured sparse autoencoder feature overlap, and examined next-token probabilities.

About me

Hi, I’m Doris Wang (王朵).

I’m an M.A. student in Computational Social Science (Economics) at the University of Chicago. I received my B.Sc. in Political Economy from King’s College London.

I study strategic interaction under incomplete information. My current public goods experiment examines how announced enforcement changes cooperation before any punishment occurs. The next project holds private reports fixed and changes the team. My operations work holds firm roles fixed and changes agent capability. The broader agenda is institutional design for settings in which AI agents hold information and act for people or firms.
