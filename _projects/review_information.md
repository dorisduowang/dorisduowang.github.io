## Research question

Are negative reviews more precise and more useful for identifying quality than positive reviews?

I reconstructed 3 preregistered experiments with 977 participants from the raw data and preregistration materials. The analysis separates preference from information value. That distinction matters because the review people want to read need not be the review that best distinguishes a good product from a bad one.

## Evidence

The independent R and Python pipelines reproduced 20 reported results. A new text analysis covered 468 reviews. Negative reviews used 15.21% cognitive-processing language compared with 10.17% in positive reviews. They also produced 3.10 correct quality judgments out of 5 compared with 2.85, even though 66% of 294 participants chose positive reviews.

The result connects to my broader agenda. Communication can be pleasant, persuasive, or informative. Institutional design depends on knowing which property the decision requires.
